## 1. How the exam asks it
Templates: **T1** step 1 (MRS, private-good benchmark) and **T4** steps 3-4 (Pareto test, second welfare theorem).

| Exam Q | Type, pts | Solution steps |
|---|---|---|
| [F25 7a](<../../Past exams/Finals/2025-10 Final (with solutions).pdf>) p.13 | Numerical, 2 | 1) MRS = MU_y / MU_x (good on the horizontal axis in the denominator). 2) Differentiate both utilities. 3) Simplify to MRS_C = 8/√y_C. |
| [F25 7b](<../../Past exams/Finals/2025-10 Final (with solutions).pdf>) p.13 | Numerical, 2 | 1) The good is *private*, so each person separately sets MRS_i = MRT. 2) Solve each equation: y_C = 4, y_D = 1. (Contrast with 7c, where the MRSs are *summed*.) |
| [R25 3a](<../../Past exams/Resits/2025-07 Resit (with solutions).pdf>) p.6 | Numerical, 2 | 1) Split the cost into an equal lump-sum tax per head (20). 2) Compare each group's benefit with its own tax. 3) Someone loses (15 < 20), so it is **not** a Pareto improvement, even if the total benefit is larger than the total cost. |
| [F24 7c](<../../Past exams/Finals/2024-10 Final (with solutions).pdf>) p.11 | Verbal, 1 | 1) Second welfare theorem: with lump-sum transfers, efficiency and distribution can be separated. 2) So build iff total benefits > total costs (23000 > 20000), and redistribute afterwards. A Pareto-improvement framing ("compensate the losers") is also accepted. |

**Pitfalls (grading schemes):** private good means MRS_i = MRT **per person**, and a public good means **ΣMRS** = MRT; mixing these up is the classic error in F25 Q7. A Pareto improvement needs *every* group to gain net of its own tax share. A positive total net benefit is not enough. "No maths in words" applies to 7c.
