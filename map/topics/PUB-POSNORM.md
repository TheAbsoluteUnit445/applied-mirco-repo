## 1. How the exam asks it
Templates: **T3** step 5 ("why intervene if Coase works?") and **T9** step 3 (normative vs positive explanation).

| Exam Q | Type, pts | Solution steps |
|---|---|---|
| [M25 2c](<../../Past exams/Midterms/2025 Midterm (with solutions).pdf>) p.2 | Verbal 1 sent., 1 | 1) Notice that efficiency is already reached (Coase). 2) So give a *distribution* reason: different MU of income or SWF weights. |
| [F25 8a](<../../Past exams/Finals/2025-10 Final (with solutions).pdf>) p.15 | Verbal ≤2 sent., 2 | 1) Normative means "what ought to be", i.e. SWF weights. 2) Link to the case: society puts a low weight on the (rich) North's welfare. Revenue or efficiency arguments = 0. |
| [F25 8b](<../../Past exams/Finals/2025-10 Final (with solutions).pdf>) p.15 | Verbal ≤2 sent., 2 | 1) Positive means "how decisions are actually made". 2) The North is a minority and gets outvoted by the majority (median voter), or corruption/bribes. (Shared with PUB-POL-VOTE/GOVFAIL.) |
| [R26 4c](<../../Past exams/Resits/2026-07 Resit (with solutions).pdf>) p.10 | Verbal ≤2 sent., 1 | Why not mandate job search even if it is efficient? Give a distributional or ethical reason (it hurts the poorest, dignity, paternalism). An efficiency argument = 0. |

**Pitfalls:** this is the most common zero in verbal parts. If the question says *normative*, or efficiency is already achieved, **only** distribution/equity/ethics arguments score. *Positive* reasons are about voting, interest groups and corruption, not about what is desirable. Keep to the sentence cap.
