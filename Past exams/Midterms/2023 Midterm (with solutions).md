a Explain why the Nash-equilibrium differs from the socially efficient outcome. Use maximally four
sentences and use the correct market failure in your explanation.
1.0 point · Open · 2/5 Page
Model answer
The socially efficient outcome is Outside, No fire. However, for Scott Fire is a dominant strategy,
such that the Nash equilibrium is Inside, Fire. Scott does not take the negative externality he
imposes on Jacinda into account when making a campfire.
+1 point

external effects.

Question 3
5.0 points · 4 questions
Company Z employs one worker. The production is described by the following function: Q = E + K,
where 0 ≤ K ≤ 1 and E is the effort chosen by the worker. The price of the product is equal to P. Note
that K is an amount of production that is realized independent of the effort of the worker. We assume
that P > 2K. The worker’s utility function is U = Y – E2 and the income Y of the worker is Y = bQ. Note
that, by assumption, there is no base salary. When the worker chooses not to work for this company,
his utility is equal to zero.
Text
a Derive the profit-maximizing bonus b. Do not only provide calculations, but also write a few
sentences along the way explaining what you are doing.
2.0 points · Open · 3/4 Page
+1 point
First derive the worker's effort. Utility is b(E+K)-E2. The first-order condition thus is b-2E=0. Solving
gives E=0.5b.
+1 point
Next consider profits. Profits equal PQ - Y. Using the production function Q=E+K and the solution for
E = 0.5b, we can write profits as P(0.5b+K) - b(0.5b+K). Taking the derivative to b gives 0.5P - b - K =
0. And so b = 0.5P - K.
b Explain in a few clear sentences why the level of the profit-maximizing bonus decreases with K.
1.0 point · Open · 1/4 Page
+1 point
It becomes more costly to increase bonus pay when K is higher, because the bonus applied to all
output, both output related to E but also output that follows from K.

Assume from now on that, in addition to the bonus b, the company also pays a base salary, denoted
by a. Hence, the employment contract now is: Y = a + bQ. The base salary can be negative.
Text
c Show that the base salary a that is needed to attract the worker is equal to -Kb-(b2)/4.
1.0 point · Open · 1/2 Page
+1 point
The outside option is zero and so the participation constraint is a + b(E+K) - E2 = 0. Substituting the
solution for E and rewriting gives a = -Kb-(b2)/4.
If you made a calculation error, no worries, you get full points if your approach is correct!
d Explain in a few clear sentences why the addition of a base salary to the contract increases the
profit-maximizing bonus b. You can derive the profit-maximizing bonus to discover the reason, but a
derivation is not necessary for the answer to be correct.
1.0 point · Open · 1/2 Page
+1 point
The base salary enables the company to extract rents from the worker. For instance, the bonus pay
that is paid for the K units of production that are not due to effort of the worker can be recouped by
reducing a. This can also be seen when writing down profits: PQ - Y = P(K+E) - A - B(K+E) =
P(K+0.5b) + Kb + (b2)/4 -b(K+0.5b). It is easy to see that the second term Kb and the first part of the
fourth term -bK cancel out. Hence, the higher cost of increasing the bonus b due to K are nullified by
the reduction in the base salary when the bonus rises.
