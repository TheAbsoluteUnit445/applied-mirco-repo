## Deck teaching sequence and graph equations

These are faithful deck inputs; short slides should link to module sections for detail.

**Optimal taxation, 12 claims:** $1$ Payments finance government; excess burden is extra lost surplus. $2$ Taxing goods and leisure uniformly changes no relative price. $3$ Taxing only goods makes leisure cheaper relative to consumption. $4$ Horizontal supply places the full price wedge on consumers. $5$ Compensated demand measures the substitution distortion. $6$ EB is quadratic in the rate. $7$ Equalise the burden of the last revenue euro. $8$ Unrelated demands yield inverse-elasticity ratios. $9$ Fixed-base revenue is an approximation. $10$ Efficiency weights differ from equity judgments. $11$ Fixed total income gives Edgeworth equalisation; work responses change that conclusion. $12$ A constant marginal rate plus a grant can have rising average rates. Sources: module §§1.1–1.6 and their exact links.

**Tax graph A:** choose constructed $P_0=10,Q_0=100$, elasticity slider $\epsilon\in[0.2,2]$, rate $t\in[0,0.3]$. Define local linear compensated demand $Q(P)=Q_0[1-\epsilon(P-P_0)/P_0]$. Then $P_t=(1+t)P_0$, $Q_t=Q_0(1-\epsilon t)$, $EB=\frac12\epsilon P_0Q_0t^2$, actual revenue $tP_0Q_t$. Bounds ensure positive quantity but can approach the revenue turning point: do not promise that revenue always increases with the rate; optionally constrain (\epsilon t<0.5). Label axes Q and euros/unit. Display current values and distinguish triangle/rectangle. Source: L6 pp.10–15; R&G pp.532–536. Constructed values differ from all exams.

**Tax graph B (optional):** linear income tax $T(y)=-\alpha+ty$, disposable income $c=\alpha+(1-t)y$, $ATR=t-\alpha/y$ for (y>0). Sliders $t\in[0,0.7]$, $\alpha\in[0,5000]$, income axis 0–30000; never plot ATR at zero. Show taxes and disposable income separately. Source: R&G “Modern Studies,” pp.544–546. This graph illustrates progressivity, not a solved optimal-tax rate.

**Political economy, 12 claims:** $1$ Explanation differs from evaluation. $2$ Lindahl shares sum to marginal cost. $3$ Sincere information and low agreement costs are crucial. $4$ Count every pairwise contest. $5$ Consistent individual rankings can cycle collectively. $6$ Agenda order matters in a cycle. $7$ Single-peakedness does not require symmetry. $8$ Median is a population order statistic. $9$ A stable majority choice need not maximise net benefit. $10$ Vote trades can help coalitions while hurting outsiders. $11$ Arrow concerns simultaneous requirements for unrestricted profiles. $12$ Producer rents are transfers until resources are consumed seeking them. Sources: module §§2.1–2.10.

**Voting graph:** constructed three equal groups $U_i(m)=a_i\ln m-m/3$, $m\in[0.5,100]$, coefficients initially 2,6,20; peaks $3a_i$; median peak 18 and aggregate maximiser $a_1+a_2+a_3=28$. Sliders should keep all peaks inside the plotted domain or explicitly show a boundary. Display peaks, population median and aggregate optimum with correct labels; do not treat arbitrary utility curves as euro willingness-to-pay unless the quasilinear specification is stated. Source: F24 Q8 p.13 for model form; these coefficients are constructed. Single-peakedness $U''=-a_i/m^2<0$. Count voter support by comparing actual utility at alternatives, never by generic distance unless symmetric utility is deliberately used.

**Rent-seeking graph (optional):** constructed $P=a-bQ$, MC (c), with (a>c>0,b>0). $Q_C=(a-c)/b$, $Q_M=Q_C/2$, $P_M=(a+c)/2$, rents $=(a-c)^2/(4b)$, DWL $=(a-c)^2/(8b)$. A dissipation fraction $d\in[0,1]$ gives total loss $DWL+d\times rents$, explicitly conditional. Graph axes Q and euros/unit. Source R&G Figure6.4 pp.210–214; original DQ6.9 has 10,0.1,2, so choose different initial values, e.g. 12,0.15,3.
