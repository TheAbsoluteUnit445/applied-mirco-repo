## Corrections and diagnosis

### 1. Monopsony and government wage setting: 4/8
1a, 3/4. Full FOC:
N'(W)Q - mu N(W) - mu W N'(W)=0.
Economic meanings: higher wages attract more employees and increase service production; higher wages raise payments to existing employees; additional recruits also have to be paid. User's three expressions are substantially present, but never assembled into the equation. Last cost is wages paid to new recruits, not another wage increase for existing employees. Issue: explicit mathematical presentation and precise interpretation, not a wrong product rule.
1b, 1/2. Higher Q raises marginal benefit at the old W. With the assumed second-order condition, optimal W rises; since N'(W)>0, employment rises. Each extra employee now produces more valuable services. User correctly identified the benefit shift, but did not state the wage/employment response. Mentioning mu does not replace the requested conclusion.
1c, 0/2. Monopsony is wage-setting power due to workers not switching infinitely readily, not simply a profitable ability to raise wages. A larger N'(W) means workers respond more strongly to wage changes and the employer has less monopsony power (holding the comparison fixed). In perfect competition the firm's labor supply is horizontal at the market wage; this does not mean N(W)=infinity. Distinguish a function's level from its slope. Conceptual gap.

### 2. Intrinsic motivation and screening: 4/6
