Exam page numbers (p.) are PDF pages of the solutions file. Templates: see [exams-tagged.md](<../_work/exams-tagged.md>) §3.

## 1. How the exam asks it
Template: **T2** steps 1 and 6 (linear market + externality: incidence is always the opening sub-question). The answer has always been **2/3 for the less elastic side**.

| Exam Q | Type, pts | Solution steps |
|---|---|---|
| [F24 6c](<../../Past exams/Finals/2024-10 Final (with solutions).pdf>) p.9 | Numerical, 1 | 1) Pre-tax equilibrium D = S. 2) Tax on **producers**: D = S + t (supply shifts up). 3) Read off P_c and P_s = P_c − t. 4) Consumers' share = ΔP_c / t = 2/3. |
| [F24 6d](<../../Past exams/Finals/2024-10 Final (with solutions).pdf>) p.9 | Verbal, 1 | Mechanism: the tax lowers what producers receive, so they cut supply along their supply curve, and the price consumers pay rises. Naming "supply elasticity" alone = 0. |
| [F25 9b](<../../Past exams/Finals/2025-10 Final (with solutions).pdf>) p.16 | Verbal, 2 | Less elastic demand: 1 pt "consumers have fewer or less valuable alternatives", 1 pt "so they substitute away less and bear a larger share". |
| [R26 3b](<../../Past exams/Resits/2026-07 Resit (with solutions).pdf>) p.8 | Numerical, 2 | Tax on **consumers**: D − t = S. Same steps; producers bear 2/3. The elasticity route (share = ε_D/(ε_S + ε_D) for producers) is also accepted. |
| [R26 3c](<../../Past exams/Resits/2026-07 Resit (with solutions).pdf>) p.8 | Verbal ≤2 sent., 2 | More elastic supply: 1 pt producers have better alternatives, 1 pt so they shift more of the burden onto consumers. |

**Pitfalls:**
- Who legally pays is irrelevant to the outcome, but it decides which curve you shift: F24 taxes producers, R26 taxes consumers.
- Check that the shares sum to 1.
- Verbal answers need the **alternatives → substitution → burden** chain (rule 3: naming = 0).
