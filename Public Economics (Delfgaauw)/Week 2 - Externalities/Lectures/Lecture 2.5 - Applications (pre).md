Common-pool (or –resource) problem can arise
when resources are Rival and Non-excluded
– Where ‘non-excluded’ is often caused by insufficiently
well-defined and enforced property rights
Rival implies that the marginal cost of more
producers / consumers is positive
Non-excluded implies that these cost are not taken
into account by individual producers / consumers
Result:
Tragedy of the commons
Common-pool problem

Why is BP > BS?
Because in deciding between working and fishing, individuals
do not take into account that the presence of their boat on
the lake reduce the quantity of fish caught by others.

 This is a negative externality!

Tragedy of the commons arises when individuals do
not take the effect of their actions on others into
account.
Elinor Ostrom (winner 2009 Nobel-prize
Economics) has shown that small communities
often set up elaborate governance schemes to
overcome the common pool problem, and can be
successful
 Coase Theorem (often with implicit contracts)
