Distinction between:
• Imperfect information
– Uncertainty faced by all parties in a transaction
– Example: future stock price company
• Asymmetric information (or private information)
– (At least) one party knows more about
1. Its own actions affecting the value of the transaction
‘Hidden actions’ leading to ‘Moral hazard’
2. A characteristic affecting the value of the transaction
 ‘Hidden characteristics’ leading to ‘Adverse selection’
Asymmetric information

 Action is efficient, and price adjusts to cover cost

Recap:
Moral hazard reduces efficiency because individuals
undertake actions that are individually rational but socially
inefficient.
So, moral hazard is about (non-verifiable) actions
In contrast, Adverse selection can arise when people have
‘private’ information on relevant given characteristics (of
people or objects)
So, adverse selection is about (fixed) characteristics (of
people or objects) relevant to the transaction
Moral hazard versus adverse selection


<!-- page 18 -->
## Page 18

18
‘Hidden characteristics’:
Some (potential) parties in a transaction have better
information about characteristics that affect the value of the
transaction than others
Consequence: uninformed people cannot offer different prices
for ‘good types’ and ‘bad types’ of trading partners with info
This can lead to adverse selection:
The informed parties self-select themselves into and out of
transactions in such a way that the uninformed side gets the
least valuable trading partners
Result:
Profitable transactions are not undertaken  inefficient.
Adverse selection

Asymmetric info may strongly reduce market efficiency
•
Moral hazard: individuals take non-verifiable actions that
are individually rational but socially inefficient
•
Adverse selection: uninformed parties interact with the
‘worst’ fraction of the parties with better info on
characteristics of transaction.
Potential private solutions to this market failure:
•
Signaling and screening. But: costly
•
Limiting possible choices / actions.
Potential public solutions:
•
Regulation (information disclosure; mandatory insurance)
•
Public production (NHS in the UK; ‘poverty insurance’)
