## 1. How the exam asks it
Template: **T9** (median voter / positive vs normative).

| Exam Q | Type, pts | Solution steps |
|---|---|---|
| [F24 8a](<../../Past exams/Finals/2024-10 Final (with solutions).pdf>) p.13 | Numerical, 1 | 1) FOC of U = 5 ln m − m/3, giving the peak m_E = 15 (0.5 pt). 2) SOC U'' = −5/m² < 0, so U has a single maximum and is single-peaked (0.5 pt). |
| [F24 8b](<../../Past exams/Finals/2024-10 Final (with solutions).pdf>) p.13 | Reasoning, 1 | 1) Order the three groups' peaks. 2) All are single-peaked, so the median voter theorem applies. 3) The median peak (m = 20) beats every alternative in pairwise voting. You get follow-through credit if 8a was wrong. |
| [F25 8b](<../../Past exams/Finals/2025-10 Final (with solutions).pdf>) p.15 | Verbal ≤2 sent., 2 | Positive explanation: the North is a minority, so under majority voting politicians court the majority who want high extraction (or corruption). (Shared with POSNORM/GOVFAIL.) |

Likely variants: show a **cycle** with a double-peaked voter; explain why the median outcome need not be efficient (it ignores the intensity of preferences: median vs mean); logrolling can raise or lower welfare.

**Pitfalls:**
- Single-peakedness needs the SOC (or a concavity argument), not just the FOC.
- Pairwise majority ≠ "the average peak".
