Exercise 3.1
A manager of a local production unit is thinking about prohibiting workers to snapchat during work
time. Snapchatting yields a utility gain expressed in euros to the workers of V per worker.
Snapchatting yields a production value loss expressed in euros to the manager of X per worker.
Assume initially that it is costless for the manager to monitor whether workers snapchat, and that
the manager can impose a non-monetary penalty on workers who do so, which is costless for the
manager, but yields a utility loss of F to the worker. We assume that F > V.
a) Is it socially efficient to allow workers to snapchat?
b) Suppose the workers earn a rent which is large enough so that they never want to quit this job.
What will the manager decide on allowing snapchatting by the workers?
c) Suppose wages are set by the manager so that workers are always indifferent between working for
the manager and working somewhere else. What will the manager decide on allowing snapchatting
by the workers?
d) Now suppose it is not so easy to monitor workers. The manager needs to invest monitoring cost C
per worker in order to detect whether workers have snapchatted. Answer questions a), b), and c).
e) How do the results change when F < V?

Exercise 3.2

Consider a worker who works for at most two periods (say, when young and when old) at a
particular firm. Each period, the worker can choose between exerting high effort and exerting
low effort. The worker’s cost of exerting high effort are cy when young and co when old. The
worker’s cost of exerting low effort are zero. The effort of the worker is difficult to observe
by the firm. With probability π, the firm observes the worker’s effort at the end of a period.
With the remaining probability, the firm does not observe the worker’s effort. The firm can
only fire the worker when it observed that the worker has shirked. In that case, the firm is also
allowed to withhold the worker’s wage for the period in which the worker was caught
shirking. When exerting high effort, the worker’s productivity is qy when young and qo when
old. When exerting low effort, the worker’s productivity is zero. The worker’s value of
alternative job opportunities is vy when young and vo when old. For simplicity, the discount
rate is assumed to be zero. We also assume that the worker cannot work elsewhere when
young and after that work at this firm when old.

a) What is the minimum level of the wage for which an old worker will choose to work at this
firm and will exert high effort? Interpret your result.

Exercise 3.3 (partly based on an Exam question from last year)
In the book a study is discussed about working from home. The study is a field experiment
(also known as a randomized controlled trial or RCT). The researchers report that the
average productivity of workers who started working from home went down. Nevertheless,
they conclude that working from home increased productivity of workers.

a) Explain in one sentence how both these things can be true at the same time. (If you write
more than one sentence, we will only consider your first sentence.)

b) A well-known problem with empirical studies using observational data is reversed
causality. Explain why reversed causality is not a problem when you run a field experiment.

Answers

3.1
a) Snapchatting increases social welfare by V – X. Hence, if V > X , it is socially efficient to allow it.
b) The manager will not allow it. This way, her payoff increases by X. The workers utility decreases by
V, but they won’t quit. The penalty F will induce the workers to comply because the detection
probability is 1 and F > V.
c) The manager will allow it if V > X. Not allowing it would raise production value by X, but increase
wages by V.
d) Allowing snapchatting now also saves on the monitoring costs and so is socially efficient if V – X > -
C. If workers earn a rent, the manager will allow it if  – X > - C, that is when C > X, i.e. if the
monitoring cost are too high. If workers do not earn a rent, the manager will allow it if  V – X > - C.
e) If the maximum penalty that can be imposed on a worker is lower than the value of snapchatting
to the worker, the worker cannot be kept from snapchatting. Hence, the manager will always allow
it, even if it is socially inefficient.

3.2
a) To induce the worker to exert high effort, it must hold that π∙0 + (1- π)wo< wo – co,
implying wo > co/ π. To make it attractive for the worker to work at this firm, the wage must
be greater than or equal to vo + co. Note that when vo = 0, then if the first condition is
satisfied, the second is as well.

b) To induce the worker to exert high effort, it must hold that π(0 + vo) + (1- π)( wy + wo –
co) < wy – cy + wo – co, implying wy > cy/ π – (wo – co – vo). To make it attractive for the
worker to work at this firm, the wage must be such that wy – cy + wo – co > vy + vo, or: wy >
cy + vy  – (wo – co – vo). Note that it depends on the values of π and vy whether one or the
other restriction is binding.

c) Use the results under a) and b). In period 2, the wage must be equal to 500. In period 1 to
300. Interpretation: In period 1 it is easier to induce high effort as more is at stake than in
period 2.

d) It is 200 + 100 = 300 in both periods.

e) The cost saving is 500 + 300 – 300 – 300 = 200. The firm’s maximum willingness to pay is
thus 200.

3.3
a) If the productivity in the control group went down even more, the conclusion is correct
that working from home increased productivity.
b) Reversed causality means that the dependent variable in a regression x may not only
affect the dependent variable y, but y may also affect x. As a result, looking at the
correlation between x and y does not tell you anything (or at least not a lot) about the effect
of x on y. For instance, in the context of personnel economics, the presence of bonus pay
may cause high performance, but it could as well be that high performance results in the
presence of bonus pay. In a field experiment the problem of reversed causality never shows
up, because the variation in x (e.g. whether or not a bonus can be earned) stems from
random assignment.
