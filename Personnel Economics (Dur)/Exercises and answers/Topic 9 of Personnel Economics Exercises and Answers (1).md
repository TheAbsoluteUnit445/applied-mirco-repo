Topic 9 of Personnel Economics: Teams

Exercises

Exercise 9.1

Consider two self-employed workers. If they work alone, each worker’s output is
given by:

Q = ei

If they work together, the team’s output is given by:

Q = β(e1 + e2)

where e1 is effort of worker 1 and e2 is effort of worker 2.

Worker’s cost of effort is ½Θe2. The value of output is p per unit. Each worker’s
utility is equal to his income minus his cost of effort.

a) What is the level of effort, income, and utility of the workers if they work alone?

Assume that when workers decide to work together, they share the value of joint
output equally.

b) What is the level of effort, income, and utility of workers if they work together?

c) Under what condition will workers decide to work together?

Suppose a social planner who aims to maximize the sum of the workers' utilities could
choose both workers’ efforts as well as whether they work together.

d) What is the level of effort, income, and utility of workers if they work alone and if
they work together? Under what condition will workers work together?

A social scientist argues that team production works better if team members care
about one another. He therefore sees a productivity-enhancing role for social activities
like going out for dinner and so on.

e) How would your results of the first part of b) change if workers care about each
other’s utility?
