## 1. How the exam asks it
Template: **T3** (Coase bargaining transfer), plus the T1 step 5 add-on.

| Exam Q | Type, pts | Solution steps |
|---|---|---|
| [M23 2b](<../../Past exams/Midterms/2023 Midterm (with solutions).pdf>) p.5 | Verbal ≤4 sent., 1 | 1) Name **both** conditions: well-defined property rights and low transaction costs. 2) Apply them to the case: only two people, the agreement is easy to verify. |
| [M25 2a](<../../Past exams/Midterms/2025 Midterm (with solutions).pdf>) p.2 | Numerical, 1 | 1) Private q = k (A's FOC). 2) Efficient q* = k − c. 3) Minimal transfer = A's lost profit = triangle ½·c·c = c²/2. |
| [M25 2b](<../../Past exams/Midterms/2025 Midterm (with solutions).pdf>) p.2 | Verbal ≤3 sent., 1 | Chain: higher c → lower q* → bigger output cut → bigger profit loss for A → bigger transfer needed. You must end with A's profit. |
| [F24 5c](<../../Past exams/Finals/2024-10 Final (with solutions).pdf>) p.7 | Numerical, 1 | 1) Bilateral efficient Q (Anne + Dikra). 2) Dikra's WTP = her gain on the **increase** from α/λ, which is d²/λ. |
| [R26 1d](<../../Past exams/Resits/2026-07 Resit (with solutions).pdf>) p.2 | Verbal ≤4 sent., 2 | Non-excludability: you can't exclude non-payers, so every beneficiary would have to be in the bargain. That means huge transaction costs and free riding on the deal. Property rights over the good can still exist (lighthouse). |

**Pitfalls:**
- M23 2b: naming only one condition = 0.
- M25 2b: "higher external cost → higher T" = 0, and "q* falls" without the link to A's profit = 0.5.
- F24 5c: paying for all Q = 0.
- "Why intervene if Coase works?" has only one scoring answer: **distribution** (see [PUB-POSNORM](PUB-POSNORM.md)).
