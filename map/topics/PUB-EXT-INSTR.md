## 1. How the exam asks it
Template: **T2** step 4 (ban vs laissez-faire). Cap-and-trade and standards have **never** been examined.

| Exam Q | Type, pts | Solution steps |
|---|---|---|
| [F24 6a](<../../Past exams/Finals/2024-10 Final (with solutions).pdf>) p.9 | Numerical, 1 | 1) A ban gives CS = PS = EC = 0, so welfare 0. 2) Laissez-faire: market Q from D = S. 3) Welfare = CS + PS − total external cost at Q (triangles, or ∫(D − S − MEC)dQ). 4) The ban reduces efficiency iff this is > 0, which gives β > 30. |
| [F24 6b](<../../Past exams/Finals/2024-10 Final (with solutions).pdf>) p.9 | Verbal, 1 | A higher demand intercept β means consumers value the good more, so **consumer surplus** is larger and the market is worth keeping despite the external cost. "Higher β → higher Q" = 0. |
| [F25 9d](<../../Past exams/Finals/2025-10 Final (with solutions).pdf>) p.16 | Numerical, 3 | 1 pt "ban gives zero CS, PS and EC". Then the surplus at the market Q is 5400 − 1800α ≤ 0, so the ban is better iff α ≥ 3. |

**Pitfalls:**
- Include **all three** components (CS, PS, *total* external cost = area under MEC up to Q, not MEC × Q unless MEC is constant).
- Verbal: name *consumer surplus*.
- Follow the direction of the inequality (when is the ban *better* vs *worse*).
