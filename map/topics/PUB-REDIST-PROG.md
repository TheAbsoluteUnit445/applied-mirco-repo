## 1. How the exam asks it
Template: **T7** (welfare programme: kinked budget constraint / job search).

| Exam Q | Type, pts | Solution steps |
|---|---|---|
| [R26 2a](<../../Past exams/Resits/2026-07 Resit (with solutions).pdf>) p.4 | Graphical, 3 | 1) Axes: income vs leisure. 2) Endpoints (0, 4000) and (200, 600). 3) Three segments: full wage, phase-out (effective wage w(1 − reduction rate)), guaranteed income. 4) Label the kinks (175, 1100) and (115, 1700). Points: 1 endpoints, 1 three segments, 1 at least one kink. |
| [R26 2b](<../../Past exams/Resits/2026-07 Resit (with solutions).pdf>) p.4 | Graphical/verbal, 2 | 1) At the old optimum (85 h), the IC is tangent to a BC with slope = the old wage. 2) Under the programme the opportunity cost of leisure there drops to 10. 3) So the IC cuts the new BC, and Bert works **less**. No calculation is needed (or possible). |
| [R26 2c](<../../Past exams/Resits/2026-07 Resit (with solutions).pdf>) p.4 | Describe method, 2 | 1) Compute the grant received. 2) Compute utility under the programme. 3) Equivalent variation = the lump sum giving that same utility at the old wage. 4) Efficiency loss = grant − EV. Naming the terms is not enough. |
| [R26 4a](<../../Past exams/Resits/2026-07 Resit (with solutions).pdf>) p.10 | Numerical, 2 | Before the benefit is introduced: 1) EU(search) = π·√(2500α) + (1−π)·√225 − c, with π = 0.8 and c = 8. 2) U(no search) = √225. 3) Search iff EU ≥ U, so π(50√α − 15) ≥ c, which gives α ≥ 0.25. 1 pt for both utilities, 1 pt for the comparison. |
| [R26 4b](<../../Past exams/Resits/2026-07 Resit (with solutions).pdf>) p.10 | Verbal ≤3 sent., 1 | A mandate forces search by people whose search cost exceeds their expected gain, a net loss (alternatively: high monitoring costs). |

**Pitfalls:**
- Graph points go to *numeric* labels (rule 13).
- "More or less?" must be argued via the slope of the IC vs the new BC at the old point.
- "Describe the steps" wants an ordered recipe with no computation (rule 10).
