## How the exam asks it

Template **T15**, plus the 1/N free-rider logic. This is the same algebra as Public's Nash vs Samuelson public-good provision (T1).

**F24 Q2 setup:** partners Arno and Bea. Revenue p(e_a + e_b). Costs ½θe_a² and ½λe_b².

| Exam Q | Type | Result |
|---|---|---|
| [F24 2a](<../../Past exams/Finals/2024-10 Final (with solutions).pdf>) | Numerical (1) | 50/50 split: e_a = p/(2θ) |
| [F24 2b](<../../Past exams/Finals/2024-10 Final (with solutions).pdf>) | Numerical (1) | Max the sum of utilities: e_a = p/θ, e_b = p/λ |
| [F24 2c](<../../Past exams/Finals/2024-10 Final (with solutions).pdf>) | Numerical (1) | Share e_a/(e_a + e_b), so Arno's income = p·e_a and e_a = p/θ (efficient) |
| [F24 2d](<../../Past exams/Finals/2024-10 Final (with solutions).pdf>) | Describe method (1) | How to check that the rule maximises welfare: compare the induced efforts with the efficient ones |
| [F24 2e](<../../Past exams/Finals/2024-10 Final (with solutions).pdf>) | Describe method (1) | Is Arno worse off? Plug **both partners'** efforts under each rule into Arno's U and compare |

**Steps:**
1. Individual FOC under the sharing rule: own share × marginal revenue = own marginal cost.
2. Planner: maximise the sum of utilities; the transfers cancel.
3. Compare. Equal sharing gives 1/N of the efficient effort, because each partner bears the full cost but gets 1/N of the return.
4. A rule that pays each partner their own marginal product restores efficiency.
5. For "describe how" questions, give an ordered recipe with no computation.

**Pitfalls:** in F24 2e you must include Bea's effort under each rule in Arno's utility. In "describe" questions, give the steps only.
