## 1. What the exam actually is

- **Evidence base:** 2 finals (Oct 2024, Oct 2025), 2 resits (Jul 2025, Jul 2026) and 2 midterms (2023, 2025). That's 123 sub-questions, all with grading schemes.
- **Question types:** about 55% numerical derivation (points per step), 35% short verbal intuition (sentence caps), about 8% "describe the steps" or empirical design, and one graph (kinked welfare budget constraint, R26).
- **Recycling is heavy.** Every midterm Personnel question and the 2025 midterm public-good question reappeared in a later resit, and F24 Q6 came back as F25 Q9 and R26 Q3. The **2025 midterm and the 2026 resit are the most likely sources** for 23 Oct.
- Each exam also has 1-2 **one-off big blocks** on a topic that's otherwise rare (teams F24, commons R25, tournaments F25, welfare programmes R26). So the B topics can't be skipped.

### How answers are graded (the 5 rules that cost the most points)
1. **Naming a term is worth 0.** Explain the mechanism (who gains, who loses, which margin changes).
2. **No "maths in words"** and respect the sentence cap (1-4 sentences).
3. **Derivations score per step.** Objective, substitute, FOC, solve. A bare answer gets 0, and an arithmetic slip with the right method gets full credit.
4. **Normative ≠ efficiency.** Once efficiency is reached, only distribution or SWF-weight arguments score. Positive reasons are voting, majorities, corruption and rent-seeking.
5. **Check corners.** Effort = 0 if b < γ, and the free rider provides 0 but still consumes.

All 15 rules: [exams-tagged.md §4](_work/exams-tagged.md).
