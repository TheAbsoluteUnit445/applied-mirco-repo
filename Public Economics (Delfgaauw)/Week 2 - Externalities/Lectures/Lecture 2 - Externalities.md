## Slide 13

13
Social gain in moving from QP to Q*
Pharma loses profit: area dcg
Growers gain from reduction in pollution: area abfe
Note: area abfe is the area below the MD curve between QP and Q*

As MSC = MPC + MD, area abfe is equal to the area between the MSC and MPC curves between QP and Q*  area abfe = area cdhg

It follows that area cdhg > area dcg
For each Q > Q*, MD > MPB – MPC

So, for each reduction in Q until Q*, gain growers > loss Pharma

Reverse holds for increase in Q up to Q* :  Loss growers < gain Pharma
Market failure: Private and social optimum

Coase Theorem
Question:

Answer:
No, under some conditions, private parties may arrive at the social optimum, without government intervention
 Coase Theorem


<!-- slide 18 -->
## Slide 18

18
Coase Theorem
Coase Theorem: if
(Transferable) property rights established & enforced, and
Transaction cost sufficiently low, then
 Private parties will negotiate towards the socially efficient outcome

Condition 1 implies that a market arises for the side-effect, so that the externality can be internalised
Condition 2 states that the cost of arriving at a mutual agreement should be sufficiently low

Note: Who owns property rights does not affect efficiency; this does affect distribution


<!-- slide 19 -->
## Slide 19

19
In the example: if Pharma has property rights on the Rhine, private optimum is QP.
However, reduction in Q benefits growers more than it hurts Pharma  room for negotiations.
(Marginal) reduction in Q makes that Pharma loses profit equal to difference between MB and MPC.
Growers benefit by MD.
Starting from QP , loss in profit is small relative to reduction in damage!
So, bargaining between Pharma and growers can improve efficiency, as growers can (more than) compensate Pharma for reduction in profit.
Coase Theorem


<!-- slide 20 -->
## Slide 20

20
What is the outcome of these negotiations?
Answer: The socially efficient level of Q: Q*
For a marginal reduction in Q, growers are maximally willing to pay marginal damage (MD)
Pharma only accepts a marginal reduction in Q if the compensation is at least the loss in profit (MB – MPC)
So, reduction in Q until MD = MB – MPC
Hence, equilibrium has MD + MPC = MB, which only holds when Q = Q*
To make both Pharma and the growers accept, the growers should pay Pharma minimally area cdg and maximally area abfe
Coase Theorem

## Slide 26

Pigouvian tax
Externality arises because prices do not reflect all social cost and benefits.  In example, for Pharma the price of pollution is 0

Solution: change prices faced by producer of externality.
 Pigouvian tax / Pigouvian subsidy

In case of a negative externality, a Pigouvian tax
is a tax on production / consumption equal to the marginal damage at the social efficient level of Q

Likewise, a Pigouvian subsidyis a subsidy for reduction in production / consumption equal to the marginal damage at the socially efficient Q

Similarly for positive externality:
Pigouvian subsidy for more consumption / production;
Pigouvian tax on a reduction in consumption / production

Pigouvian tax
Pigouvian tax in the example:
Facing tax t, Pharma sets output such that MB = MPC + t
When t equals MD at Q*  , Pharma chooses Q such that
MB = MPC + MD(Q*)
	which only holds at Q = Q* , the social efficient level of Q!
