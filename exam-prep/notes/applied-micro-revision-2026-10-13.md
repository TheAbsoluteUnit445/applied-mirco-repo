## Week 1: public-good Nash equilibrium

Requested follow-up: revisit how to recognise and solve corner equilibria.

- The user can derive individual first-order conditions but needs practice understanding why substituting two interior best-response equations may produce a contradiction.
- Explain that a contradiction rules out both players contributing positively; it does not invalidate substitution or rule out Nash equilibrium.
- Practise identifying the higher desired total, testing the other person's contribution at zero, and verifying both best responses.
- Contrast these cases with log-private-consumption models where both players can contribute positively. Do not teach higher valuation implies sole provision as a universal rule.
- Reinforce purchases versus consumption: both consume total public provision, including a person purchasing zero.
- Reinforce that subsidy changes the buyer's price, while physical marginal production cost remains unchanged.

Example reviewed: benefits 24 ln Q and 12 ln Q, unit cost 6. Efficient Q = 6; private Nash purchases (4, 0); subsidy 2 gives purchases (6, 0), achieving efficient provision under the stated financing assumptions.
