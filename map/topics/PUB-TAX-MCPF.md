## 1. How the exam asks it
Template: **T4** step 1 (costs include the tax *plus its EB*). The lecture flags this as the "old exam exercise" type.

| Exam Q | Type, pts | Solution steps |
|---|---|---|
| [F24 7a-7b](<../../Past exams/Finals/2024-10 Final (with solutions).pdf>) p.11 | Numerical, 1 + 2 | 1) EB of the financing tax (½·t·ΔQ = 5000). 2) Social cost of the project = revenue 20000 + EB 5000, i.e. MCPF = 1.25 here. 3) Compare with the (weighted) benefits, giving α > 1.25. (See [PUB-CBA](PUB-CBA.md).) |
| [R25 3b + 3e](<../../Past exams/Resits/2025-07 Resit (with solutions).pdf>) p.6 | Related | 3b computes an EB; 3e appraises a lump-sum-financed project. Know why a lump-sum-financed project has no EB term. |

Possible verbal variant: "Why might a project with benefits > budget cost still lower welfare?" Because each euro raised by a distortionary tax costs society 1 + MEB > 1 (MCPF ≈ 1.2-1.5).
Criteria for tax systems (efficiency, egalitarianism, intergenerational equity, stabilisation) have not been examined.

**Pitfalls:** forgetting the EB in the project cost (the F24 7b trap).

## 4. Book (source of truth)
- **MUST-READ** R&G 15.2 Excess Burden Measurement with Demand Curves, pdf p. 509-510. EB ∝ t², so marginal EB per extra dollar of revenue rises steeply. This is the economics behind the MCPF.
- READ R&G 16.1 "The Ramsey Rule", pdf p. 532-535. Marginal EB per euro of revenue (shared with RAMSEY).
- SKIM R&G 16.5 Other Criteria for Tax Design, pdf p. 548-561. Horizontal equity, administration costs, evasion: the book's version of "criteria for judging tax systems".
- SKIM R&G 15.4 Does Efficient Taxation Matter?, pdf p. 521.
