Arrow’s result explains why no voting rule has every desirable property.

With at least three alternatives, unrestricted preferences, complete/transitive social rankings, unanimity, independence of irrelevant alternatives and no dictator cannot all be guaranteed.

Single-peakedness restricts the preference domain. It avoids a particular difficulty without disproving the impossibility result.

Collective decision-making · 10/13

Public employees’ implementation discretion can influence spending.

Officials supply useful expertise and institutional memory. Vague legislation also gives them discretion over policy design and implementation.

If officials value reputation, power or patronage, their preferred spending may exceed the level where marginal social benefit equals marginal cost. This is a conditional mechanism, not a claim about every bureau.

Small organised groups can have stronger lobbying incentives than dispersed taxpayers.

Concentrated benefits make participation worthwhile for each beneficiary. Small individual losses make opposition less worthwhile.

Collective decision-making · 12/13

Government growth has competing explanations with different implications.

Rising income and service prices, crises followed by inertia, and redistributive voting incentives are distinct mechanisms. No single theory explains all government growth.

A positive answer must apply an actual mechanism to the scenario. A normative answer must state the welfare reason separately.
