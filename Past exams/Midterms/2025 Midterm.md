Exercise 3 Personnel economics
A proﬁt-maximizing tobacco company hires a worker. The worker’s utility function is: U = w −1
2θe2 −βq,
where w is the worker’s income, e is the amount of effort exerted by the worker, and q is the amount of
output produced by the worker. θ and β are positive parameters. The worker’s income consists of a bonus
b per unit of production. There is no base salary. Hence, w = bq. The production function is q = µe. The
ﬁrst term of the utility function reﬂects that the worker enjoys income. The second term reﬂects worker’s
cost of effort. And the third term reﬂects that the worker feels guilt about producing tobacco, which
reduces his utility. The worker chooses to work for the tobacco company if his utility is at least equal to
zero. Effort e cannot be negative.
2p
3a
Derive the level of effort the worker chooses to exert.
