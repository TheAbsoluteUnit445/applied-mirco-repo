Question 1
Suppose there are two individuals, Anne and Ben, who both derive utility from the consumption of a public
good and from the consumption of a private good. Anne’s marginal rate of substitution between the
public good and the private good is given by
MRSA =
xA
GA + GB
where xA is the number of units of the private good that Anne consumes, GA is the number of units of
the public good bought by Anne, and GB is the number of units of the public good bought by Ben. Ben’s
marginal rate of substitution is given by
MRSB =
xB
GA + GB
where xB is the number of units of the private good that Ben consumes. Marginal production cost of
both goods are constant and equal to their price on the market. The price of the public good equals pG.
The price of the private good equals px = 1. As a result, the marginal rate of transformation between the
public good and the private good equals pG. Anne and Ben have the same level of income y, which they
spend fully on the two goods. Given px = 1, this implies that xA = y −pGGA and xB = y −pGGB.
1p
1a
Consider the assumptions of this exercise. How can you see in this exercise that the public good is
non-rival while the private good is rival?

Question 3
Company Z employs one worker. The production is described by the following function: Q = E + K, where
0 ≤K ≤1 and E is the effort chosen by the worker. The price of the product is equal to P. Note that K is an
amount of production that is realized independent of the effort of the worker. We assume that P > 2K. The
worker’s utility function is U = Y - E2 and the income Y of the worker is Y = bQ. Note that, by assumption,
there is no base salary. When the worker chooses not to work for this company, his utility is equal to zero.
