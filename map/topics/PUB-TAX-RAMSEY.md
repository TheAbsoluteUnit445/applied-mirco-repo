## 1. How the exam asks it
Template: **T5** (Ramsey / inverse elasticity, plus progressivity).

| Exam Q | Type, pts | Solution steps |
|---|---|---|
| [R25 3c](<../../Past exams/Resits/2025-07 Resit (with solutions).pdf>) p.6 | Numerical + rule, 2 | 1) Compute ε_X = |dQ/dP·P/Q| at the untaxed equilibrium: 5/7 (1 pt). 2) Compare with ε_Y = 2. 3) Inverse elasticity rule: t_X/t_Y = ε_Y/ε_X > 1, so t_X is higher (1 pt). |
| [R25 3d](<../../Past exams/Resits/2025-07 Resit (with solutions).pdf>) p.6 | Verbal ≤4 sent., 2 | Elastic demand means good substitutes, so a tax causes a large quantity response, which means a large EB per euro of revenue. To minimise total EB for a given revenue, tax elastic goods at lower rates. |
| [F25 10a](<../../Past exams/Finals/2025-10 Final (with solutions).pdf>) p.18 | Numerical relations, 2 | 1) t_i ∝ 1/ε_i. 2) With ε = 3, 1, 1/3: t_R = t_M/3 = t_P/9. Only ordering the rates with EB-elasticity reasoning gets 1 of 2. |
| [F25 10b](<../../Past exams/Finals/2025-10 Final (with solutions).pdf>) p.18 | Verbal 1 sent., 1 | The poor's inelastic good is taxed most, so the ATR falls with income: **regressive** (see [PUB-TAX-PROG](PUB-TAX-PROG.md)). |

**Pitfalls:**
- Compute the elasticity **at the pre-tax equilibrium point**. Use dQ/dP = 1/(dP/dQ) if demand is given as P(Q).
- Give the *ratios* when asked for "relations", not just the ordering.
- The verbal part needs the substitutes → quantity response → EB chain.
