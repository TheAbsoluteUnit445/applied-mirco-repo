## 1. How the exam asks it
Template: **T1** step 5 (Coase fails for public goods because of non-excludability).

| Exam Q | Type, pts | Solution steps |
|---|---|---|
| [M23 1a](<../../Past exams/Midterms/2023 Midterm (with solutions).pdf>) p.2 | Verbal, 1 | 1) Look at the MRS functions. 2) G enters as G_A + G_B in *both* utilities, so it is non-rival (my consumption doesn't reduce yours). 3) x_i only enters i's own utility, so it is rival. |
| [R26 1d](<../../Past exams/Resits/2026-07 Resit (with solutions).pdf>) p.2 | Verbal ≤4 sent., 2 | 1) Name **non-excludability** as the characteristic that breaks Coase. 2) Mechanism: you cannot exclude non-payers, so bargaining with every beneficiary is impossible and any agreement can't be enforced. Transaction costs explode (free riding on the deal). 3) Property rights over the good itself can still exist (the lighthouse example). |

**Pitfalls:**
- M23 1a: arguing via different prices/costs (p_G vs 1) = 0. Read rivalry off the *utility/MRS*.
- R26 1d: listing both characteristics and concluding = 0. The scheme warns that property rights are *not necessarily* violated.
