## 1. How the exam asks it
Template: **T6** (congestion / commons: Nash vs efficient use).

| Exam Q | Type, pts | Solution steps |
|---|---|---|
| [R25 1a](<../../Past exams/Resits/2025-07 Resit (with solutions).pdf>) p.2 | Numerical, 2 | 1 pt: in Nash, travel times are equal across modes (40 = 25 + f(C)). 1 pt: solve C = 2000. |
| [R25 1b](<../../Past exams/Resits/2025-07 Resit (with solutions).pdf>) p.2 | Num + verbal, 4 | 1) Total time T = C·t_car(C) + (N − C)·t_bike. 2) FOC dT/dC = 0, giving C = 1000 < 2000. 3) 2 pts verbal: each driver ignores the extra delay imposed on *other* drivers (negative externality, tragedy of the commons). |
| [R25 1c](<../../Past exams/Resits/2025-07 Resit (with solutions).pdf>) p.2 | Numerical, 2 | Recompute Nash under each policy. Road expansion: induced demand, Nash time stays 40, so 0 saved. Bike lanes: the outside option improves for everyone, saving 25,000 min. |
| [R25 1d](<../../Past exams/Resits/2025-07 Resit (with solutions).pdf>) p.2 | Verbal ≤3 sent., 2 | With a binary choice only the relative cost matters, so a car tax ≡ a bike subsidy for efficiency. (Shared with PIGOU.) |

**Pitfalls:**
- Efficient = minimise **total** time (marginal, not average, cost of a car).
- Policy effects must be recomputed through the *Nash* condition, not the efficient one.
- 1b verbal is worth half the points: say "others" and "doesn't take into account".
