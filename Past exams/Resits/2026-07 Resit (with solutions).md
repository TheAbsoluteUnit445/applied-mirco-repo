2 PTS · OPEN · 1/2 PAGE
Which of the characteristics of a pure public good makes that at least one of the
conditions of the Coase Theorem is always violated? Explain why. Use no more than four
sentences.
Model answer
- Best answer: A pure public good is non-excludable. This makes it impossible (= too costly) to
enforce a contract where people promise to contribute to (pay for) the provision of the public good
if they consume it. Hence, it violates the condition that transaction cost should be low.
- Alternative answer: A pure public good is non-excludable. This makes it impossible to enforce
property rights, in the sense that an owner of a public good cannot prevent other people from
using it (for free).
- Alternative answer: A pure public good is non-rival. This makes that all individuals that value
the public good should consume it. The transaction cost of bargaining with many individuals is too
large.
Note: it is possible that pure public goods have an owner (think of a lighthouse or a dike). Hence,
the condition that property rights should be assigned is not necessarily violated in case of public
goods.
