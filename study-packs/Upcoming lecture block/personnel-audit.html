Algebra and source qualifications

  • F24: derived private efforts p/(2θ), p/(2λ); efficient efforts p/θ, p/λ; cancellation of endogenous sharing factor gives income peₐ and peᵦ. Derived Arno's utility difference p²(3λ−2θ)/(8θλ) and checked it with four independent numerical substitutions, including the zero boundary at θ=1.5λ. θ>λ alone does not imply he loses. These extra calculations are independently derived; official Q2d/e request only a method.
  • F24 information distinction: the proposed share depends on individual effort. It is outside chapter 24's maintained constraint that only joint output is contractible. Fixed unequal shares and effort-proportional shares must not be conflated.
  • Group bonus: explicit comparison gives optimal gap-filling when d≤√(2B); with colleagues' 3.7, target five and bonus one, effort 1.3 gives utility 0.155. Boundary H=5−√2≈3.5858 supports two best responses at equality. A rendered original textbook PDF p.472 visually confirms the radical √2; only the text layer/Markdown conversion drops it. The source is retained unchanged.
  • DQ24.3: log-benefit voluntary provision uniquely fixes total Q=1, not an individual allocation. Equal contributions 1/N require symmetry. The textbook's individual-ratio wording should not be treated as uniqueness of all Nash contribution vectors.
  • Fettuccine: increments 20,25,35, cost90, rates5/4/3 give follower gains10/10/15. Ability costs70/90/110 with rate4 give10/10/30. These are original book numbers, explicitly identified.
  • Binary weakest links: for N>1 and D/N>1/2 both all-work and all-shirk are equilibria. Binary perfect substitutes instead have one-volunteer pure equilibria; all-shirk is not an equilibrium under that same profitability bound. Coordination failure in observed behavior is not synonymous with a pure equilibrium.
  • Original Kuhn PDF p. 526, Result26.7 and footnote21, use “complements” where the surrounding substitutes section and calculations require substitutes. Original PDF text confirms this is not just the Markdown conversion. This is disclosed rather than silently rewriting the source.
  • Chapter27 selection fixes Eᵢ=1. Full-productivity-pay and partial-productivity-pay answers retain that assumption. DQ27.1a does not specify how incumbent arbitrary shares are reallocated on admission; the solution supplies an explicitly conditional proportional-dilution case instead of claiming a unique universal answer.
  • Discrimination empirical examples retain population, setting and measured outcome. The combat-testing numbers in DQ16.1 are the book's expressly hypothetical figures; 50% more callbacks is a relative difference; orchestra and BTB findings are the book's historical studies, not current policy or legal advice.
