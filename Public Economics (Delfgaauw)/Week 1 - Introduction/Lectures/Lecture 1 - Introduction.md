## Page 27

Problems in private provision of public goods
Non-excludability implies:
•
People benefit from public goods even without contributing
 no-one can be ‘forced’ to pay for consumption of public
good provided by others

So, everyone prefers that others pay for public goods
Non-rivalness implies:
•     If someone contributes, other people benefit
•
But: when contributing, people may not (fully) take into
account that others also benefit from their contribution
Result: Free rider behaviour, leading to underprovision.
 Market failure

Problems in public provision of public goods
Is provision by the government easy solution?
No!
1.     Government does not know individuals’ preferences.

 If ‘society’ pays, individuals have incentive to exaggerate
their (marginal) valuation of the public goods

 Overprovision!

 (theoretical solution: preference-revelation mechanisms)
2.    Government must raise taxes to finance public goods provision

 Lowers efficiency through tax distortions  (week 5-6)
