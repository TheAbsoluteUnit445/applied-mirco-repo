# PUB-SWF: Social welfare functions (utilitarian, Rawls, weighted), CBA with SWF weights

**Status:** taught (W1, W4) | **Exam weight:** 3 of 4 finals/resits (F24, R25, F25) plus midterm M25. Split weight 10.8% summed, **about 2.7% per exam**. It shows up in 4 of 6 papers, often as a 1-2 pt add-on | **Priority: A**

Exam page numbers (p.) are PDF pages of the solutions file. Templates: see [exams-tagged.md](<../_work/exams-tagged.md>) §3.

## 1. How the exam asks it
Templates: **T4** (weighted-SWF project appraisal / redistribution), **T3** step 5 and **T9** step 3 (normative reason = distribution).

| Exam Q | Type, pts | Solution steps |
|---|---|---|
| [F24 7b](<../../Past exams/Finals/2024-10 Final (with solutions).pdf>) p.11 | Numerical, 2 | 1) List each group's benefit. 2) The rich pay revenue 20000 **plus EB 5000** (from 7a). 3) Weighted sum: α·(poor benefit) + 1·(rich benefit − 25000) > 0. 4) Solve: α > 1.25. |
| [R25 3e](<../../Past exams/Resits/2025-07 Resit (with solutions).pdf>) p.6 | Numerical, 2 | 1) Net amount per group = benefit − lump-sum tax (the poor get 15 − 20 < 0). 2) Weight the poor by α and the rich by 1. 3) Sum > 0 gives **α < 2**. The poor are net losers, so a *higher* weight works against the project. |
| [F25 6a](<../../Past exams/Finals/2025-10 Final (with solutions).pdf>) p.11 | Numerical, 2 | 1) max w·U_A + U_B s.t. I_A + I_B = Y. 2) FOC: w·MU_A = MU_B. 3) With √-utility, solve I_A = w²/(w²+β²)·Y. |
| [F25 6b](<../../Past exams/Finals/2025-10 Final (with solutions).pdf>) p.11 | Verbal ≤2 sent., 2 | 1 pt: a higher w means society values A's utility more. 1 pt: so the marginal social value of giving A income rises, and more is redistributed to A. |
| [F25 8a](<../../Past exams/Finals/2025-10 Final (with solutions).pdf>) p.15 | Verbal ≤2 sent., 2 | Normative reason = a low SWF weight on the (rich) North. Efficiency arguments = 0. (Shared with PUB-POSNORM.) |
| [M25 2c](<../../Past exams/Midterms/2025 Midterm (with solutions).pdf>) p.2 | Verbal 1 sent., 1 | Coase already gives efficiency, so the only reason left is distribution (different MU of income or SWF weights). "Correct market failure" = 0. |

**Pitfalls:**
- Forgetting the EB in the cost of a tax-financed project (F24 7b trap).
- Not checking who the net losers are (R25 3e).
- Normative questions score **only** distribution/ethics arguments (rule 5 in exams-tagged §4).
- 6b wants the economic channel, not the formula restated.

## 5. Book discussion questions
- **MUST 3.10** ([ch 3](<../../Textbooks/Rosen & Gayer (md)/03 - Tools of Normative Analysis.md>), assigned W4). Split $300 under a given SWF (the formulas are lost in the md, so open the PDF). *Hint:* substitute I_J = 300 − I_M, set the weighted MUs equal, check the corner.
- **MUST 3.11** (not assigned). 400 lbs of food, utilitarian vs equal-utility split. *Hint:* utilitarian equalises MU; equal-utility sets U_T = U_W; compare who gets more and why.
- **MUST 12.2** ([ch 12](<../../Textbooks/Rosen & Gayer (md)/12 - Income Redistribution- Conceptual Issues.md>), not assigned; MU formulas in the PDF). *Hint:* MU_S = MU_C with I_S + I_C = 100. Extreme weights give a corner. Constant MU gives a corner or indeterminacy.
- **MUST 12.7** (not assigned). T/F on three SWFs. *Hint:* separate weights on utility from MU of income. Equality is optimal only with equal weights and identical concave U.
- **MUST 8.5** ([ch 8](<../../Textbooks/Rosen & Gayer (md)/08 - Cost-Benefit Analysis.md>), assigned W4). Subway vs legal services, break-even weight. *Hint:* compute both NPVs, then set NPV_subway = λ·NPV_legal and solve for λ.
- USEFUL 3.6 (W1, social indifference curves), 12.6 (maximin).
